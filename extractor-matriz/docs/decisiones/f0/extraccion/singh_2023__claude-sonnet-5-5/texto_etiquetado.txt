[p01-b06 | titulo] Abstract
[p01-b07 | parrafo] This study is focused on an empirical investigation of the relationship between organizational support perception (POS) and organizational citizenship behaviour (OCB), and also to find out the efect of employee happiness (EH) as a mediating variable on this relationship. The data was obtained from 219 retail sector employees through the survey questionnaire. The data was analyzed with the use of structural equation modeling (SEM) carried out in AMOS 26 to test the hypotheses. The study’s findings pointed out that POS predicts OCB both directly and indirectly through the mediating role of EH. The results of this study suggest that management should strive to improve the factors that influence employees’ POS to raise EH at work and OCB. Human resource practices such as flexible work arrangements, training and development opportunities, wellness programs, workplace justice, etc. can contribute to strengthening the relationship between the organization and its employees, thereby enhancing the perception of organizational support.
[p01-b08 | parrafo] Keywords Perceived organizational support · Employee happiness · Organizational citizenship behaviour · Retail sector
[p01-b09 | titulo] Introduction
[p01-b10 | parrafo] In the prevailing dynamic and competitive environment, it is crucial for all organizations that their entire workforce exhibit extra-role behaviour (Grego-Planer, 2019). The term extra-role behaviour was coined by Organ in the 1980s which is also called organizational citizenship behaviour (OCB). OCB can be explained as behaviour that is crucial for an organization’s eficient functioning but does not fall under a specific job responsibility. Without any expectation of compensation or credit, employees take on extra responsibilities voluntarily with the sole intent of assisting their coworkers and the company (Smith et al., 1983). The success of an organization is significantly impacted by an employee’s selfless eforts for the betterment of the organization. An organization’s competitive advantage is generated from its personnel’s positive behavior and endeavors that go beyond their mandated or contractual responsibilities (Ranjhan & Mallick, 2018). Therefore, organizations must identify the variables that could have an impact on OCB. Organizations should be aware of the drivers of OCB in advance and strive to enhance these drivers so that employees will be encouraged to involve in extra-role behaviour.

[p02-b02 | parrafo] Perceived organizational support (POS) tends to drive organizational citizenship behaviour in the workplace. POS promotes extra-role behaviour that benefits the entire organization, such as advocating for the organization’s goods & services and making insightful suggestions for any problem or projects which ultimately improves the operation of the company (Kaufman et  al., 2001). The findings of past studies indicated that POS has been positively associated with job performance, affective commitment, employee engagement, job satisfaction, and organizational citizenship behaviour (Alshaabani et  al., 2021; Erdogan & Enders, 2007; Piercy et  al., 2006). Furthermore, social exchange theory (SET) also stresses that POS invokes the reciprocity principle, as supported employees build a positive image of the organization and actively contribute to the attainment of organizational goals by voluntarily participating in extra-role behaviours.
[p02-b03 | parrafo] Enhancing employee happiness (EH) at work is very crucial for organizations to fully utilize their human capital (Van De Voorde et al., 2012). Happy employees seem to be gregarious, amiable and they contribute more to contextual performance. Positive organizational results are projected to grow in businesses with happy employees and it is also believed that happy employees would take fewer actions that endanger the organization’s success. Generally, employees who are happy at work are less likely to involve in counterproductive work behaviours (A. Singh & Banerji, 2022; Thompson & Bruk-Lee, 2021). Maintaining happy employees may be extremely useful in terms of lowering the attrition rate as a high attrition rate may lead to reduced productivity, high replacement cost, and dysfunctional organizational cultures (Staw, 1980). Hence, EH is necessary to keep business on track.
[p02-b04 | parrafo] EH is necessary to keep business on track. The Indian retail industry contributes significantly to the country’s economy, making  up about 10% of its GDP and providing employment to over 40 million people. It is the  fifth-largest retail market globally, and it is expected to reach US $1.5 trillion by 2030,  further emphasizing its importance to the country’s economy (IBEF, 2022). This growth  of India’s retail industry has increased the demand for skilled personnel, emphasizing the  need for effective HR systems, policies, and practices to meet the industry’s rapidly grow- ing demands (Singh & Mishra, 2008).
[p02-b05 | parrafo] HR plays a vital role in retail, as employees who deal with customers are perceived as the face of the organization. Recognizing the importance of HR in the growth and development of India’s retail sector, this study examines the relationship between POS, EH, and OCB. However, research on the link between POS and OCB through EH in the Indian context, under the social exchange theory, is still limited. Therefore, this study aims to empirically explore POS as a predictor of EH and OCB to address this gap.

[p03-b01 | titulo] Literature Review
[p03-b02 | titulo] Perceived Organizational Support
[p03-b03 | parrafo] POS is described as employees—“global beliefs about the extent to which the organization cares about their well-being and values their contributions” (Eisenberger et al., 1986). POS includes workplace justice, supervisor’s support, good working conditions, and organizational rewards which ultimately determine how well employees will accomplish their inrole tasks (Rhoades & Eisenberger, 2002). If employees have a propitious view about these factors, it will cultivate a sense of belongingness with the organization. Furthermore, POS stimulates desirable results such as increased dedication, trust, and positive attitude (Yu & Frenkel, 2013). As a result of POS, employees may feel obligated to help the business in achieving its objectives. The employee’s positive perception of management support would result in a social exchange relationship between the company and its employees, inspiring them to act in ways that the company values (Yu & Frenkel, 2013).
[p03-b04 | titulo] Employee Happiness
[p03-b05 | parrafo] Albert Schweitzer opined that “success is not the key to happiness, happiness is the key to success” (Thompson & Bruk-Lee, 2021). Despite this prevalent notion, EH is usually considered as an insubstantial topic by corporate entities, immaterial to the bottom-line results. Many positive organizational outcomes are likely to be derived from happy employees for example job satisfaction, organizational commitment, and lower turnover rate (Thompson & Bruk-Lee, 2021). Happiness is a multifaceted notion that encompasses psychological well-being, positive emotions, and overall satisfaction with work and life (Diener, 2000; Fisher, 2010; Grant et  al., 2007). EH has been influenced by a variety of factors, which can be clubbed into four broad categories i.e., personal factors, factors related to family environment, organizational and social factors (Dutschke et  al., 2019; Espasandín-Bustelo et al., 2020). Past studies have identified social support available at work (Drummond et  al., 2017), leadership style (Perko et  al., 2016), training & development opportunities and resourceful environment (Xanthopoulou et al., 2012) as the organizational factors that linked employee happiness.
[p03-b06 | titulo] Organizational Citizenship Behaviour
[p03-b07 | parrafo] The OCB was initially characterized as “individual behavior that is discretionary, not directly or explicitly recognized by the formal reward system and that in the aggregate promotes the efective functioning of the organization”(Organ, 1988). Employees who believe they are "citizens" of their organizations, willingly adopt initiatives that assist not only their coworkers but the organization as a whole (Grego-Planer, 2019). Workers’ OCBs improve teamwork and aid in promoting organizational productivity (Lin & Peng, 2010). According to Williams and Anderson (1991), OCB can be classified into two major dimensions: Citizenship behaviour towards the individual (OCB-I) and towards organizations (OCB-O). OCB-I is interpreted as ofering support to co-workers related to their assigned tasks and exhibiting empathy while they are experiencing personal problems. To help the organization as a whole, an employee engages in OCB-O. For instance, proving innovative solutions to the issues, adhering to rules & regulations, and contributing to the successful implementation of the strategy. Employees who exemplify these behaviours are treated as valuable assets of organizations and are a source of competitive advantage.

[p04-b02 | titulo] Theoretical Foundation and Hypotheses Development
[p04-b03 | parrafo] The current study examines POS, EH and OCB all within the framework of social exchange theory (SET). SET has been frequently deployed to understand the mutual interactions that have been developed between employees and the organization. According to (Blau, 1964) positive behaviour from others are more likely to be reciprocated by employees. In an organization when an employer treats the workforce fairly, praises their eforts, and genuinely is concerned about their well-being, employees feel a deep sense of gratitude and thus, strongly encourage to reciprocate (Dawley et al., 2008). To repay their organizations, employees tend to exhibit positive work attitudes such as higher performance, extrarole behaviours, and increased organizational commitment (Aryee et  al., 2002). Such an ideal scenario can only be achieved when employees at a company abide by the exchange’s norms and regulations (Cropanzano & Mitchell, 2005). SET claims that a healthy relationship between employees and managers/ supervisor fosters a mutual understanding that creates a collaborative work environment that would be advantageous for both employees and the organizations (Cole et al., 2002). Applying SET, this research synthesizes the literature on perceived organizational support, employee happiness, and organizational support to formulate the following  hypotheses. The conceptual model for this research is shown in Fig. 1.
[p04-b04 | titulo] Perceived Organizational Support and Employee Happiness
[p04-b05 | parrafo] Past studies have repeatedly highlighted the benefits of having an employee that feels supported by organizations (Gaëtane Baran et al., 2012; Caesens et al., 2017; Kurtessis et al., 2015). Particularly, POS was found to be strongly related to diferent desirable employment outcomes namely, work engagement, loyalty, & employees’ subjective well-being and negatively associated with inimical work behaviour such as intention to quit (Eisenberger & Stinglhamber, 2011). It is believed that POS satisfies a number of socioemotional needs such as esteem need, emotional support, need for afiliation, etc. which in turn improves employees’ well-being (Kurtessis et  al., 2015). Happiness or well-being at work is crucial for fostering employees’ optimal performance and minimizing absenteeism, and poor physical & mental health (Panaccio & Vandenberghe, 2009). Additionally, it has been determined that POS is associated with reduced levels of psychological stress among workers, higher job satisfaction & happiness, and favourable attitudes toward their organization (Caesens & Stinglhamber, 2014; Panaccio & Vandenberghe, 2009).

[p05-b08 | parrafo] Happiness is seen to have a beneficial impact on workers and  triggers positive behavioural changes (Lyubomirsky et al., 2005). Happy workers are found to be more altruistic, empathic, and visibly have greater OCB (Avey et  al., 2008; Barsade & Gibsonk, 2007; Spector & Fox, 2002). Happiness or Positive emotions promote deeper connections, relationship development, social integration, and the formation of more efective social interactions (Diener & Seligman, 2002; Waugh & Fredrickson, 2006) which may lead to the courteous, compassionate, and sympathetic behaviour of employees (Rego et  al., 2010). Happy employees are always inclined to assist their organization regardless of their formal roles and responsibilities (George, 1991). In the same vein, Williams and Shiaw (1999) suggested that positive emotions at work encourage extra-role behaviour (OCB), which benefits organizations and improves organizational results. [p06-b01 | titulo] Employee Happiness as a Mediator Between Perceived Organizational Support and Organizational Citizenship Behaviour
[p06-b02 | parrafo] Happiness at work has a substantial and profound impact on the employees’ productivity, quality of life and job satisfaction (Bhattacharjee & Bhattcharjee, 2010; Boehm & Lyubomirsky, 2008; Marescaux et al., 2019). According to George (1991) employees who perceive afective well-being at work, engage in more prosocial conduct. Happy employees are more inclined to have favourable opinions of their workplace and coworkers, which promotes additional role engagement (S. Williams & Shiaw, 1999). POS has been identified as one of the predictors of employee happiness. POS amplifies the feeling of positivity among employees by strengthening their perceptions that the company values their work and performance (Rhoades & Eisenberger, 2002). POS ensures that assistance will be provided by the employer as needed to efectively perform one’s task and handle challenging circumstances, which promotes the psychological well-being of employees. SET can also be used to comprehend the mediating role of employee happiness in the link between POS and OCB. According to SET, in a work environment where employees feel supported by their organization, they experience better sentiments and moods, resulting in higher levels of happiness, which may lead to organizational citizenship behaviour.

[p06-b04 | parrafo] H14: EH mediates the relationship between POS and OCB.
[p06-b05 | titulo] Research Methodology
[p06-b06 | parrafo] A standardized questionnaire was used to gather the data from small and medium retail sector employees working in Gorakhpur City (Uttar Pradesh, India). Gorakhpur city of eastern Uttar Pradesh, India, is known for its rich cultural and historical significance and it has become a thriving commercial and trading center. With a population of 673,448 people in 2011, it was designated as a metropolis area in 2021 (Wikipedia, 2023). The city ofers a vast and lucrative consumer base for the retail industry, with several shopping malls and standalone stores ofering local and international brands in various categories. Its proximity to major industrial and commercial hubs makes it an advantageous location for setting up a retail business.
[p06-b07 | parrafo] A convenience sampling method was adopted to distribute 350 survey forms to employees who were either full or part-time employed in supermarkets, departmental stores and shopping malls. A total of 247 responses were received out of which 219 were found to be valid (duly filled). Table 1 depicts the respondents’ demographic characteristics.
[p06-b08 | titulo] Measures
[p06-b09 | parrafo] The survey instrument was designed utilizing scales that had already been validated from prior research. It was divided into two sections: (1) participants demographic details and (2) measures of the current study factors. A nominal scale was used to assess demographic factors whereas POS, EH, and OCB were measured on a Likert 5-point scale, that ranges from strongly disagree (1) to strongly agree (5). Table 2 depicts the details of Study’s measures.

[p12-b04 | titulo] Discussion
[p12-b05 | parrafo] The goal of this research work was to ascertain the efect of POS on extra role performances. Additionally, this research aimed to identify the efect of EH on this interaction. Consistent with SET, the study’s findings highlighted that POS positively influences OCB confirming the outcomes of earlier studies (Alshaabani et al., 2021; Kaufman et al., 2001; Piercy et al., 2006). Personnel who perceive high POS levels feel obligated to reciprocate the company for its favours by going above and beyond their job roles. However, this outcome contradicts earlier research that was unable to establish any significant direct relationship between POS and OCB (Jehanzeb, 2020). Based on SET, Cropanzano and Mitchell, (2005) stated that employees are expected to conform to the norms of reciprocity, which means that if they perceive that their organization is treating them well, they will be motivated to exhibit positive behaviors. Thus, in consistency with SET, the finding of this research work state that POS creates a positive exchange between employees and organizations leading to increased OCB (Chiaburu et  al., 2015). When employees perceive that the organizations prioritizes their well-being, employees are more likely to involve in discretionary behaviors that benefit the organization. This exchange promotes a positive work environment, reinforces trust, and encourages sustained positive behaviors, ultimately facilitating OCB and contributing to a collaborative work culture and heightened productivity.

[p14-b05 | parrafo] The study’s outcomes have practical implications for the organizations as they are understanding the benefits they can reap by providing support to workers. The findings of this study show that to achieve better OCB, firms should concentrate on fostering happiness at work. This can be done by ofering increased organizational support, which is considered as a major antecedent of EH and OCB. Organizations must ensure that they provide appropriate working conditions, wellness programs, flexible work arrangements, health care, and safety measures for their employees. Organizations are also recommended to respect employees’ viewpoints and eforts and assist them in developing their skills and capabilities for promoting the happiness of employees and maximizing extra-role performance. Furthermore, managers should  formulate  and implement POS-oriented human resource policies. The likelihood that employees will exhibit OCB increases with the extent of assistance they receive from their employers. [p15-b02 | titulo] Conclusion
[p15-b03 | parrafo] Despite the growing interest in POS, EH and OCB in today’s highly volatile and competitive environment, only a few research have looked at the interaction between POS and OCB in the presence of EH as a mediator in the Indian context. Overall, the research’s findings indicate that POS decreases employees’ perceptions of being regarded as the only means for achieving the objectives of the organization by meeting their physiological, social, and esteem needs. POS make employees aware that they are valued, recognized, and accepted as a member of organizations which triggers increased levels of happiness among employees. Happy employees are more inclined to indulge in extra-role performance. SET also posits that positive social exchange relationships result in increased loyalty and OCB towards their organizations.
[p15-b04 | titulo] Future Research Directions
[p15-b05 | parrafo] Although the current research has its merits, it also has certain limitations that need to be acknowledged and taken up in future research work. Firstly, researchers should gather data from diferent locations and industries to enhance the generalizability of the study’s results. This would give us a better idea of whether the relationships observed in the study are consistent across diferent contexts. Additionally, researchers can investigate how demographic factors, such as gender, experience, and age, might afect the link between POS and OCB. By studying these variables, researchers could gain a better understanding of how diferent groups of employees perceive and respond to POS and how this afects their willingness to engage in OCB. Finally, future research could explore other factors beyond EH that could impact the relationship between POS and OCB, such as organizational trust, psychological capital, and organizational commitment. By examining these factors more closely, researchers can gain a more complete understanding of what drives OCB and identify other ways to encourage OCB in the workplace.
